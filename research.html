Research

Three threads run through the lab's projects, each grounded in atomic-resolution imaging rather than bulk measurements alone.

Fig. 1 — Quasiparticle interference map

Unconventional superconductivity

Reading pairing symmetry off the atomic lattice

We use quasiparticle interference and spectroscopic imaging with scanning tunneling microscopy to probe the pairing symmetry of unconventional superconductors. Recent work has focused on the spin-triplet candidate UTe₂ and on sign-changing s-wave order in monolayer Fe(Se,Te)/Bi₂Te₃ films, where the superconducting gap structure carries direct information about the underlying pairing mechanism.

Topological quantum materials

Surface states you can watch respond in real time

Spin-resolved tunneling and time-resolved spectroscopy of topological Kondo insulators and antiferromagnets, including how their protected surface states respond to magnetic fields and to ultrafast optical driving — for example, Floquet–Bloch control of the Dirac gap in a topological antiferromagnet, and axionic tunneling signatures in a Kondo insulator.

Emergent charge & spin order

Watching order form, melt, and defect

Correlated electron systems host charge density waves, spin excitations, and topological defects that emerge and compete with superconductivity. We've imaged magnetic-field-sensitive charge density waves in UTe₂, long-lived spin excitations near domain walls in 1T-TaS₂, and cascades of magnetic order in the layered antiferromagnet GdTe₃ — tracking how these orders form, melt, and interact at the atomic scale.
