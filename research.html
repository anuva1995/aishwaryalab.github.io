What we work on

Research

Three threads run through the lab's projects, each grounded in atomic-resolution imaging and quantum sensing rather than bulk measurements alone.

Fig. 1 — Quasiparticle interference map

Unconventional superconductivity

Reading pairing symmetry off the atomic lattice

We use spectroscopic imaging and quasiparticle interference with scanning tunneling microscopy to read pairing symmetry directly off the atomic lattice. Our work spans candidate spin-triplet superconductors such as UTe₂, where we have mapped the low-energy electronic structure through quasiparticle interference, and engineered superconducting films such as monolayer Fe(Se,Te)/Bi₂Te₃, where we identified a robust, sign-changing s-wave order parameter. Together, these studies show how the superconducting gap structure itself encodes the underlying pairing mechanism.

Topological phenomena

Probing surface states through spin-selective tunneling

We probe the surface states of topological materials, including Kondo insulators and antiferromagnets, using spin-resolved tunneling to see how symmetry-protected states respond to magnetic fields. This has included developing spin-selective tunneling techniques using SmB₆ nanowires and uncovering axionic tunneling signatures in a topological Kondo insulator.

Intertwined charge and spin orders

Watching order form, melt, and defect

Strongly correlated electron systems host charge density waves, spin excitations, and topological defects, sometimes intertwined with superconductivity, sometimes emerging on their own. Our work has examined how the charge density wave in UTe₂ responds to and melts under applied magnetic field, how long-lived spin excitations form near domain walls in 1T-TaS₂, and how a cascade of magnetic orders develops in the layered antiferromagnet GdTe₃, tracking how these orders form, melt, and interact at the atomic scale.
